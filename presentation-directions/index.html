Three ways in.

These are visual direction studies for the talk, using the game’s own materials: actual fields, particles, grids, silhouettes and typography. Each route handles the same range of content so the comparison is about the system.

My recommendation: Field Study. It has the closest continuity with MASSIVE and leaves room for scientific references without adding another visual language. Use Big / Small’s dramatic title-to-footage cuts for the opening and closing. Cosmic Atlas is the stronger alternative if the personal story and archival imagery lead the talk.

Use the arrows or thumbnails to explore. Switch direction to keep the same screen. “Play sample” opens a silent game recording; Escape returns to the study. PowerPoint files contain editable type and placed stills; use the included game fonts to preserve typography. Motion is demonstrated here, not embedded in the PowerPoint studies.

Field Study

Black, white, Input Mono. A steady 64-pixel margin. One observation at a time. Images behave as evidence: clearly framed, named and then released into full-screen play.

Best for explaining what changes and why it matters to the player. Keep small catalogue labels for held frames; remove them when the event needs the whole screen.

What the game
already gives us

A field you can read through contact

The steady orthographic view, shared plane and sparse black-and-white actors establish a readable world. The grid is a continuous spatial reference. Its deformation makes relationships visible while the player supplies a stable point of orientation. Wireframe enemies, opaque bodies and coloured interiors carry different jobs; they should not be averaged into one luminous treatment.

Colour with a consequence

ORBITAL’s violet-to-amber density, DYNAMO’s layered magnetic curves, HIGGS’s topographic contours and COSMOS’s crisp particle structure belong to distinct systems. EMERGENCE uses solid, unlit species colours. The deck preserves those differences. No added glow, artificial grain, scanlines or global colour grade is needed.

One cause, several visible effects

In ORBITAL, the density function informs both distribution and encounter probability. Resonance’s authored curves organize rendering, contact and grid influence. DYNAMO’s changing shelter boundary coordinates the field presentation and exposure rules. SINGULARITY keeps a continuous surface through a change of view and connection. The presentation should reveal that coupling through one complete event, then a held detail.

Motion needs a beginning and a rest

Show the reference first. Tell the audience where to look. Play the game event once. Hold the final state while Matt explains the consequence. A quiet image is part of the timing. The review uses prerecorded media so the accepted game appearance remains intact and presentation playback does not depend on a live simulation.

Coverage and final capture needs

ORBITAL, DYNAMO, COSMOS and EMERGENCE were recorded from the current checkout, commit 27a3e7ce, on 15 October. Scripted inputs operate the real controllers. Earlier isolated clips are retained as visual specimens, with their provenance in the PowerPoint notes. The current screenshots establish appearance; they are not a substitute for the final complete storm, strike, feeding or passage sequences.

The next production pass needs Matt’s signed COSMOS book photograph and signature detail, an oblique view of the flat player among dimensional objects, a matched calm-to-storm DYNAMO sequence, and final traversal and feeding clips. The nine screens here are system tests, not the entire 18-minute running order. The local editorial outline is used as a working brief; its personal story inputs remain provisional.

Keep the kinds of science visible

The Chladni photograph is an experiment. NASA’s magnetosphere image is a conceptual animation. ORBITAL is an orbital-inspired game distribution, rather than a calibrated atomic model. DYNAMO’s active storm field is authored; recorded SWMF/BATS-R-US output is a separate reference prototype. SINGULARITY’s passage is an authored geometric interpretation. COSMOS and EMERGENCE preserve selected relationships while adding explicit game rules.
